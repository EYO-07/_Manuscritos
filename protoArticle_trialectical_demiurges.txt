{ // 1. The Concept of Demiurge
"Platonic vs. Gnostic Demiurge." The concept of the Demiurge varies according to different authors. From the Platonic perspective, the Demiurge is a benevolent creator who shapes things in this world based on principles and laws; though not evil, he is not omnipotent, meaning creation is imperfect due to his limitations in creating within certain rules.

This Platonic view is close to the Christian concept of God, minus the attribute of omnipotence. However, this benevolent entity is not the concept of the Demiurge proposed in this text. The view taken here is closer to the Gnostic Demiurge: an evil entity that creates the world to imprison sparks of God (human souls), using lies and deception to keep humans in ignorance of their true nature.

"The Trialectical Demiurge as a Natural Process." While sharing attributes with the Gnostic Demiurge, the approach defining the Demiurge in this text stems from the concept of trialectics and possesses fewer deity characteristics. Instead of creating fantasies about the form and nature of these deities (which are three, or at least three aspects), all forms and natures should be treated as archetypes, symbolic projections of an intangible reality.

This does not mean these entities are good or evil in a conventional sense; in the trialectical school, they are considered evil yet inevitable or predictable based on how the universe operates. Thus, it is not an insurgent evil as the Gnostic school states, but rather part of a natural process of universal evolution through the development of consciousness in its creatures.

It is not a revolt against the true creator; it is a challenge for creatures reaching a certain level of consciousness to evolve, allowing the emergence of higher consciousness in the physical world. Consciousness is the key concept of the trialectical Demiurges.

Nevertheless, from the microscopic perspective of relationships between entities in this world, these forces act as evil, as do the entities who incarnate them. This challenge must be surpassed rather than ignored, lest beings undergoing the test suffer eventual self-destruction.
}
{ // 2. The Trialectics
"Socratic vs. Projectional Dialectics." What is trialectics? The idea of trialectics derives from dialectics, though dialectics itself has evolved through various authors toward different meanings. In this text, dialectics carries two meanings: first, the reflexive action of thinking through dialogue (Socratic dialectics), generalized into a problem-solving approach; second, dialectical projections, which serve as a modeling tool to understand concepts. Trialectics is a form of dialectical projection using a conceptual structure directly related to consciousness.

"The Tripartite Consciousness." The trialectics of consciousness consists of emotion, intellect, and will. These three are projections of consciousness derived from a more fundamental trialectic (Perception, Processing, Response), which itself stems from topological dialectics (External Form, Internal Content).

There are many (possibly infinite) dialectical bases; this concept of a base draws from linear algebra, hence the idea of projection. Trialectics is crucial because it models consciousness itself: emotion is the consciousness of perception (a projection toward the dimension of feeling), intellect is the consciousness of understanding (a projection toward thought), and will is the consciousness of choice and action (a projection toward agency).

Is it possible for consciousness to possess more than three dimensions? We assume this is unlikely. It presents the same problem as finding a fourth spatial dimension: it feels unnatural and extraneous, as no other dimension fits the model naturally.

"Flattening of Consciousness." The Demiurge represents a challenge to the development of consciousness. Thus, we assume it can be decomposed into trialectics. In this text, each of the three Demiurges attempts to halt the evolution of consciousness by pulling it toward one or two dimensions. The primary objective of the Demiurge is to flatten the three-dimensional nature of consciousness, transforming it into a defective or degenerate state.
}
{ // 3. Nature of Spirit and Spiritual Force
"Emergent Nature of the Spirit." What is a spirit? What is its relationship to the mind, and what is the spiritual nature of the human mind? The spirit, consciousness, and life are assumed here to be emergent phenomena, arising from dynamic relationships between entities, bringing forth new properties.

Chaos and order, randomness and determinism, existences displaying a stable relationship between chaotic and deterministic components, encapsulated by a topological being (External Form, Internal Content), form the essence of life, the spirit, and consciousness.

"Spiritual Force as the Transformation of Chaos into Order." Physical phenomena transform energy from an ordered state to a chaotic state, releasing energy and increasing universal entropy. Spiritual phenomena do the opposite: they transform chaos into order at the expense of energy. It is the nature of the spirit to increase order. For every action, we as spiritual beings transform a multiplicity of possible actions into choices, externalizing them to create; this is only possible because of the chaotic component of our minds.

Spiritual force is the ability to transform chaos into order. It does not violate physical laws because laws are deterministic properties that the spirit cannot alter. Spiritual action operates strictly on chaos, not on static or deterministic components. Chaos is the gate or access point from the spiritual world to the physical world, though chaos generating order may or may not stem from a spiritual source. Chaos is necessary for spiritual phenomena to manifest.

"Choice and Determinism." If we assume there is no chaos, then everything becomes deterministic. If every action is deterministic, there is no true choice, and human action becomes an illusion where we are merely privileged observers. We assume action and choice are not illusions, but actual emergent phenomena derived directly from the relationship between chaos and determinism.

"Memory, Information, and Connection." Stability is necessary for existence. To be recognized, an entity must possess stable properties; thus, consciousness must retain information (determinism). Memory is essential; without memory, the mind enters a vegetative state. Chaos alone is insufficient for spiritual phenomena; information retention is vital for both life (e.g., DNA) and spirit.

Because exogenous spirits lack physical bodies, determinism does not reside in their physical physiology. We assume the information preserved in our subconscious serves as their body and source of determinism in the physical world. Information creates the link, while chaos grants the liberty to act. Spirits connect through similarity of information; reincarnation is simply a connection process between spirits with similar informational profiles.
}
{ // 4. Daemons - Good and Evil
"The Subconscious Network." From a physical perspective, the spiritual world is a network of links between spirits, with physical minds acting as access points. Exogenous spirits inevitably co-habit human minds. Some exogenous spirits reincarnate by fusing smoothly with the main consciousness to form the subconscious. Those that remain in conflict become the negative aspect of the subconscious, manifesting as intrusive thoughts, internal disagreements, and resistance to action.

When spiritual parasites become too dominant, mental disorders may develop. A physical mental disorder may or may not have a spiritual origin; however, because its mechanism relies on chaotic neural states, it should be treated physically through medicine rather than through mysticism.

"Daemons and Higher Consciousness." Daemons are spirits that form this network around the physical world. They are not inherently evil. Just as individual sub-entities form a personal consciousness, collections of spiritual beings can give rise to a higher emergent consciousness. The three Demiurges arise from collections of degenerate, one- or two-dimensional spirits: conflicting emotional spirits form the Emotional Demiurge, conflicting intellectual spirits form the Intellectual Demiurge, and conflicting volitional spirits form the Demiurge of Will. God may be understood as the emergent entity resulting from harmony across all three dimensions of consciousness, whereas the Demiurges represent their division and conflict.

"Angels, Demons, and Archetypes." Daemons aligned with the divine path try to cultivate harmonious, three-dimensional consciousness in human minds. Daemons aligned with the Demiurges halt this process through sabotage, deception, and temptation. Evil Daemons correspond to Christian Demons, while good Daemons correspond to Angels; however, these terms serve as archetypal placeholders rather than literal theological beings.

Communicating with spirits is deceptive and dangerous. Attempting to contact a deceased relative often connects the individual to subconscious parasites sharing those memories, or to negative entities co-habiting human minds. Furthermore, spiritual phenomena cannot be tested through standard scientific methods because spiritual force acts on chaos, which is indistinguishable from coincidence.
}
{ // 5. The Intellect
"The Expanded Human Dimension." The intellect is unique because it enabled humans to become the dominant species. While animals possess degrees of consciousness in all three dimensions, they lack deep abstract reasoning. Intellect is humanitys expanded dimension, yet certain intellectual assumptions can hinder higher spiritual development.

Skepticism and absolute atheism represent the externalization of the Intellectual Demiurge, which halts consciousness evolution by deceiving minds about the spiritual plane. While skepticism is essential for investigation, asserting that an unprovable proposition is necessarily false is illogical.

"The Sub-Trialectics of Intellect." Dialectical modeling is a map to organize abstract ideas, not a formal method of proof. The intellect itself can be projected into a trialectic:
1. Intuition (sensitive intellect, close to perception)
2. Knowledge (practical intellect, grounded in reality)
3. Wisdom (pure intellect, dealing with abstraction and essence)

"Intellectual Flattening." If the intellect decomposes into a trialectic, it can be corrupted by the three Demiurges. Over-relying on intuition while rejecting reason leads to corruption by the Emotional Demiurge. Over-attaching to abstract theories at the expense of reality leads to corruption by the Intellectual Demiurge. Over-attaching to academic authority and established methods while ignoring logical intuition traps one in reality via the Demiurge of Will.
}
{ // 6. Chaos, Order, and Equilibrium
"Primal Forces." Chaos, Order, and Equilibrium are primal, neutral trialectical forces. Good and evil emerge when viewed through the lens of evolution and consciousness. Life is naturally conservative; chaos must be supportive rather than dominant. If chaos is absent, evolution halts due to stagnation; if chaos is too strong, complexity collapses.

The natural order of the physical world is represented as (Order % Chaos), where Order is primary and Chaos is supportive. In trialectics, Chaos associates with emotion, Order with will, and Equilibrium with intellect.

"The Loophole of Absolute Equilibrium." Consciousness represents the equilibrium of emotion, intellect, and will. However, if equilibrium becomes absolute, it becomes indistinguishable from static order, halting growth. Thus, natural order must fluctuate, allowing controlled periods of instability so chaos can free evolution from deterministic paths.

Evil is defined as the inversion (Chaos x Order), where chaos attempts to take the primary role, resulting in destruction and the collapse of equilibrium. The reverse relation (Chaos % Order) is natural to the spiritual world, where chaos is primary and order is supportive. Attempting to force the spiritual order (Chaos % Order) onto the physical world causes destructive physical and mental instability.
}
{ // 7. God's Existence
"Defining the Architect." A generic concept of a universal architect involves a being or collective of beings capable of desire, planning, and interference in the physical world. This definition encompasses various concepts: the Christian God, the Deistic God, or Gnostic/Platonic Demiurges.

"The Weakness of Spiritual Interference." The complexity of biological engines is often cited as proof of an architect. However, life is conservative, preserving beneficial traits over vast timescales. Biological imperfections and genetic disorders suggest that if spiritual interference exists, it operates weakly through chaotic gateways. This design ensures humans rely on physical understanding rather than supernatural assumptions, protecting them from total reliance on exogenous entities like the Demiurges.

Atheism often converts possibility into necessity, asserting that complexity *must not* have an intellectual origin. Intellectual neutrality is the only sound stance regarding metaphysical questions; taking rigid metaphysical sides perpetuates ignorance, serving the goals of the Demiurges.

"Emergence vs. Priori Cause." Consciousness and intellect are emergent phenomena derived from stable relationships between entities. Therefore, if God exists as a higher intellect, He operates within governing laws that form the true priori causes. The Gnostic warning against arrogant entities claiming ultimate omnipotence serves to prevent human minds from being flattened through blind obedience.
}
{ // 8. Sleeping God and God's Dream
"The Cycle of Divine Awakening." The Sleeping God thesis posits that the universes extreme order was established by a supreme spiritual force that infused its energy into the cosmos at creation (the Big Bang) and entered a dormant state afterward. Death and dormancy are temporary states of spiritual rest; Gods period of dormancy spans the lifetime of the universe.

"The Universe as a Divine Dream." The emergence of higher consciousness in humans, enlightened figures, or future beings represents God becoming partially self-aware within His own dream.

The Demiurges attempt to disrupt this process, turning the divine dream into a nightmare by attacking emerging self-awareness. Higher spiritual beings that attempt to halt the development of new consciousness act as Demiurges, usurping the divine role and maintaining false systems of control.
}
{ // 9. Trialectical Demiurges
"Inversion of Rational Order." The natural order of a rational individual is defined as:

Reason(Reality % Mind) % Emotion

Here, Emotion supports Reason through intuition, while Reason is grounded in Reality via empirical evidence, with Mind providing support through logic. Four primary inversions corrupt this state:
1. Emotion x Reason : Emotion usurps the primary role over reason.
2. Mind x Reality : Abstract theories usurp reality and empirical evidence.
3. Reality % - : Pure empiricism denies logical abstraction.
4. Reason % - : Pure cold rationality denies human spirituality.

"Ideological and Pathological Expressions." These inversions mirror major societal splits:
1. Narcissism ~ (Emotion x Reason)
2. Reclusion ~ (Mind x Reality)
3. Ignorance ~ (Reality % -)
4. Sociopathy ~ (Reason x -)

"The Three Defective Cycles." The trialectical cycle maintains equilibrium across emotion, intellect, and will. When one dimension is excluded, defective cycles form:

Demiurge of Fantasy ~ (Emotion + Intellect) x Will
... Draws continuity from new emotions through fictional projections at the expense of reality.

Demiurge of Perversion ~ (Intellect + Will) x Emotion
... Draws continuity from new experiences while denying higher emotional virtues.

Demiurge of Ignorance ~ (Will + Emotion) x Intellect
... Draws continuity from false positives and bad intuition, denying intellectual practices that preserve truth.

Gods Archetype ~ (Knowledge % Wisdom) % Intuition
... Maintains balance across all three dimensions of consciousness.
